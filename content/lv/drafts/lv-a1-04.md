
## Part A — Latvian only

1. Šodien ir sestdiena, un Rīgā ir skaists laiks.
2. Šodien es runāju tikai latviski. Ne angliski!
3. Es ceļos pulksten astoņos.
4. No rīta es vienmēr gribu kafiju ar auzu pienu.
5. Bet man nav auzu piena. Tad es eju uz veikalu.
6. Veikalā es pērku burkānus un sieru.
7. Bet auzu piena nav!
8. Ak nē. Tā ir skumja dzīve, kā teica Pēteris, haha.
9. Es pērku govs pienu. Tas nav tas pats.
10. Pusdienlaikā es gatavoju burkānu zupu.
11. Man ļoti garšo burkānu zupa.
12. Tad Jānis raksta: "Vai mēs mācāmies kopā?"
13. Es atbildu latviski. Tas prasa laiku, bet es rakstu latviski!
14. Pēcpusdienā es eju uz darbu. Es strādāju kafejnīcā Vecrīgā.
15. Tur es gatavoju kafiju un tēju.
16. Kafejnīcā es runāju latviski ar visiem.
17. Bet viens klients runā ar mani angliski.
18. Es atbildu latviski. Viņš atkal runā angliski.
19. Tas ir grūti. Es gribu runāt latviski!
20. Pēc darba man ir brīvs laiks.
21. Laiks ir labs, un es skrienu mežā.
22. Es domāju par Pēteri. Vasarā viņš spēlē volejbolu Jūrmalā.
23. Varbūt es arī braucu uz Jūrmalu. Man patīk jūra.
24. Vakarā es cepu kūku un lasu grāmatu latviski.
25. Es saprotu tikai mazliet, bet man ļoti patīk mācīties.
26. Šodien es runāju latviski visu dienu.
27. Tikai viens cilvēks runā angliski — bet tas neesmu es!
28. Pulksten desmitos es eju gulēt.
29. Tāda ir mana sestdiena Rīgā.

## What changed

- **The day now has a purpose** (s2): only Latvian, no English. Everything that follows either tests that rule or serves it, so the monologue has a spine instead of being a list.
- **The oat milk fails her** (s5–9). She goes to the shop _because_ she's out, the shop doesn't have it, and she uses Pēteris's line on herself: `Tā ir skumja dzīve!` The callback works because the learner has heard it before, and buying cow's milk is a small, funny defeat.
- **Jānis's message** (s12–13) pays off the L01 loop. `Vai mēs mācāmies kopā?` is the exact thread they left hanging. She answers in Latvian — slowly — which is the rule holding.
- **The customer who switches to English** (s17–19) is her want, dramatized. This is the lesson's real conflict: she answers in Latvian, he keeps going in English, and she can't do anything about it.
- **Pēteris made explicit** (s22–23). Running in the forest reminds her of him, and his Jūrmala invitation from L03 is finally something she considers accepting.
- **The ending earns its shrug** (s26–27). She kept the rule all day. The one exception was someone else's English, not hers — a small, satisfied joke rather than "that's my Saturday."

Grammar targets unchanged: `pulksten` + locative numerals, sequencing with `tad` and `pēc tam`, parts of the day.

## Flags for the native reviewer

1. **`Ne angliski!`** — is this how you'd say "not in English"? Or does it need `Ne vārda angliski` / another form?
2. **`Bet auzu piena nav!`** — genitive after negated existence, subject-first. Confirm word order.
3. **`Tas nav tas pats`** — confirm this is the idiom for "it's not the same".
4. **`Jānis raksta: "Vai mēs mācāmies kopā?"`** — present tense as a proposal in a message. Confirm it reads as "shall we" and not "are we".
5. **`Es atbildu latviski`** — confirm `atbildēt` with a language adverb.
6. **`ar visiem`** — instrumental plural of `visi`. Confirm.
7. **`viens klients runā ar mani angliski`** — confirm `ar mani` placement.
8. **`Es domāju par Pēteri`** — `par` + accusative, and the accusative form of Pēteris. Confirm.
9. **`Laiks ir labs`** — `laiks` means both "time" and "weather". Confirm it reads as weather here, given `brīvs laiks` (free time) appears in s20.
10. **`visu dienu`** — accusative of duration. Confirm.
11. **`bet tas nav es`** — confirm the pronoun form in this construction.
12. **`Tāda ir mana sestdiena`** — confirm; carried over unchanged from v1.
