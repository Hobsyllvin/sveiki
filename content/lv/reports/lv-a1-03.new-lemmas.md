## Gloss changes to existing entries (5)

These reuse an approved lemma but needed a second sense. Each is a change to already-reviewed
content and should be checked:

- **`cik`** — added `how`. `cik` was approved only as `how-much` (from `cik ilgi` = how long).
  Before an adjective or adverb it means plain "how": `Cik skumja dzīve!` (s10), `Cik jauki!`
  (s26, s33). Forcing `how-much` would have produced "how-much sad life".
- **`labs`** — added `best` for the superlative `vislabākā` (s11). `vis-` + comparative +
  definite ending is an inflection of `labs`, not a new lemma.
- **`vēl`** — added `else`. Approved as `still`; `Ko vēl tu dari?` (s31) is "what else",
  and "what still you do" would misread.
- **`kāds`** — added `some`. Approved as the interrogative `what-kind` (s18 still uses that);
  `kādu instrumentu` (s31) and `kādu dienu` (s40) are the indefinite "some".
- **`tas`** — added `those` for the fem. nom. pl. `tās` (s43). Also given `seeAlso: ["tā"]`
  because the new adverb lemma `tā` is syncretic with `tas`'s feminine forms.

## Sentences where I had doubts

1. **s1 `satiekas`** — `satikties` is reflexive in form but *reciprocal* in meaning ("meet
   each other"), not reflexive ("meet oneself"). R5 covers reflexives and has an escape hatch
   for lexicalised non-reflexive meanings, but does not name the reciprocal case. I glossed
   `meet` and noted "refl. form, reciprocal". **R5 arguably needs a reciprocal clause.**
2. **s11 `pīlītes`** — a diminutive (`pīle` "duck" → `pīlīte`). **No rule covers diminutives.**
   I chose `little-ducks` over `ducklings` to keep the diminutive visible, consistent with the
   method's literalism, but this is my invention and needs either a ruling or a rule.
3. **s20/s21 `auzu`, s37 `burkānu`** — genitive-plural noun modifiers. I glossed them as bare
   English plurals (`oats`, `carrots`), giving "with oats milk" and "carrots cake". The
   alternative is a singular gloss (`oat`, `carrot`), which reads better but hides the plural.
   No rule covers genitive modifiers; `siera` and `govs` are singular so they don't show the
   problem. **Worth a ruling since compound genitives are everywhere in Latvian.**
4. **s25 `braukt`** — means "to go by vehicle". `iet` is already `to-go` and `ceļot` is
   `to-travel`, so I used `to-ride`, which is a slight distortion: Emma is more likely taking a
   train than riding. The honest alternative is a hyphenated `to-go-by-vehicle` (legal under R1).
5. **s27 `priekam`** — dative of purpose ("for fun"). R4 grants the `to-` gloss only to
   *dative experiencers*, so I followed the rule literally and glossed bare `joy` with note
   "dat. of purpose". The decode line reads "I run only joy", which is rough even by this
   method's standards. **R4 may want a purpose-dative clause.**
6. **s30 `tā`** — in `tieši tā` this is the adverb "so/thus", not the demonstrative `tas`.
   The forms are identical, so I created a separate `tā` lemma and cross-referenced it. A
   native speaker should confirm the split is right rather than treating it as `tas`.
7. **s36 `kūkas`** — an elliptical one-phrase question ("Siera kūkas?"). The case is
   genuinely ambiguous (nominative or accusative depending on the elided verb), so the note
   says only "pl.; case ambiguous (elliptical)" rather than guessing.
8. **s45 `priecājos`** — glossed `rejoice` to stay person-free per R2. "am-glad" would read
   more naturally but smuggles the person into the gloss.
9. **s2 `Prieks tevi redzēt`** — `prieks` is glossed `joy` per its existing entry, so the line
   reads "joy you to-see". Consistent with the approved `prieks iepazīties` treatment, but
   worth confirming the pattern still reads as intended.

## Constructions R1–R15 did not cleanly cover

Four gaps, in rough order of how often they will recur:

1. **Genitive noun modifiers** (`siera kūka`, `auzu piens`, `pludmales volejbols`,
   `govs piens`, `burkānu kūka`) — no rule says whether to gloss the modifier as a singular
   or preserve its number. Five instances in one lesson.
2. **Diminutives** (`pīlīte`) — no rule at all.
3. **Reciprocal reflexives** (`satikties`) — R5 covers reflexive and lexicalised-reflexive,
   not reciprocal.
4. **Non-experiencer datives** (`priekam`) — R4 only licenses `to-` for experiencers, leaving
   purpose datives with a bare gloss that reads badly.

I did not add rules for these, per the instruction not to touch R1–R15 without a decision.
